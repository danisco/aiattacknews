1Password says attacker copied customer account data and vaults stayed secure

Names and sign-in email addresses were among the records copied. The company says customers can keep their passwords and Secret Keys and should watch for unexpected account recovery messages.

AI Attack News · Published November 25, 2026

An attacker copied account records for some 1Password customers after gaining access to an employee’s corporate GitHub account, the password manager said on November 25. The company said the attacker could not access the passwords, notes or other items customers keep in their vaults.

What information was copied?

1Password began investigating on November 19. It said the copied records included names, email addresses, account identifiers and the dates accounts were created and last used. It did not give a total number of affected customers.

The records also included tokens from certain invitation and account recovery emails. Those tokens alone could not open an account. 1Password said it directly notified the small group of customers who had such a process pending and found no internal evidence of account takeovers.

What customers should watch for

1Password says its services are operating normally. It advises customers to be wary of invitations and recovery requests they did not initiate and to open the app or type its web address themselves. The company said it disabled the compromised employee account, restored the affected service and rotated or revoked internal credentials that might have been exposed. Its disclosure gives no evidence of AI involvement.
