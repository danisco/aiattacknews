Bell says breach exposed customer data across five Canadian telecom brands

Bell is contacting residential and business customers. Account and payment details may have been exposed, though Bell says credit card and bank account numbers were unaffected.

AI Attack News · Published November 23, 2026

Bell Canada said on Monday that someone broke into an internal system on November 17 and obtained personal information about some customers of Bell, Bell Aliant, Bell MTS, Virgin Plus and Lucky Mobile. Spokesperson Ellen Murphy told The Canadian Press that phone and other customer services continued throughout the incident.

What customers may have lost

Bell’s public notice lists contact details such as names, addresses, email addresses and phone numbers. It also lists account numbers, services purchased and the date and amount of a customer’s last payment. Bell has not said how many people were affected.

Those details could help someone impersonate a Bell representative. Bell says it will never contact customers to ask for a password, PIN or bank account information. Customers can check the company’s public notice directly if they receive a suspicious message.

What Bell said about AI

Murphy described the intruder as an “agentic threat actor.” Bell has not identified a model or described which actions an AI system took. The company says it cut off the intruder’s access, continues to monitor its systems and has involved the Royal Canadian Mounted Police’s cybercrime unit.
