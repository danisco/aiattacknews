CISA adds medical imaging flaw used in university attack to federal remediation list

Amazon traced a university server takeover to AI agents using a flaw in MONAI Label. CISA’s catalog lists November 26 as the remediation date and calls for forensic checks.

AI Attack News · Published November 23, 2026

November 24 update · Federal deadline

On November 23, the U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency added the MONAI Label flaw, CVE-2026-133968, to its catalog of vulnerabilities known to have been exploited. The entry lists November 26 as the remediation date under the federal directive. CISA instructs covered agencies to follow the vendor’s guidance and check affected systems for signs of compromise. The applicable deadlines depend on how exposed a system is.

The catalog entry does not identify additional victims. Amazon’s account concerns university servers in East Asia. Whether anyone obtained patient information remains unknown.

What the activity records show

Amazon reconstructed the September 8–10 campaign from records left on the operator’s server. Two efforts to gain access failed, including one that lasted roughly 16 hours. On a server running MONAI Label, the agents found a previously undocumented flaw and later gained full control, Amazon reported on November 20.

The records also show where a human intervened. The operator subsequently directed the agents into another university network. Amazon inferred how the operator gained that foothold. Its account does not establish whether anyone obtained patient or research data.

What maintainers have fixed

The MONAI Label maintainers published a critical advisory on November 18. It identifies versions through 0.8.5 as affected and links the correction in the source code. The advisory lists version 0.8.6 as patched. PyPI published that release on November 22.

Correction, November 23, 2026. An earlier version said the advisory did not name a patched release. The maintainers identify version 0.8.6.
