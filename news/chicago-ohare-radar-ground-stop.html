O’Hare ground stop lifted after radar problem delays inbound flights

The FAA canceled its program delaying Chicago-bound flights. By 7:47 p.m. Central it listed general airborne arrival delays of 15 minutes or less.

AI Attack News · Published November 24, 2026

The FAA paused flights bound for Chicago O’Hare International Airport on Tuesday after a problem with the radar system used to track aircraft and vehicles on runways and taxiways. Flights leaving Chicago continued to depart.

November 24 evening update · Arrival delays ease

After lifting the ground stop, the FAA canceled the separate program that spaced out Chicago-bound departures. Its cancellation notice said O’Hare could use three arrival runways even though the surface surveillance equipment was still out of service at the time.

At 7:47 p.m. Central, the FAA listed no destination-specific delays for O’Hare. It put general airborne arrival delays and departure gate or taxi delays at 15 minutes or less. Those airport-wide figures describe conditions at that time. Travelers can check individual flights with their airline.

What changed for travelers earlier Tuesday

By 4:07 p.m. Chicago time on November 24, the FAA had lifted the ground stop, ABC7 Chicago reported. That allowed inbound aircraft to start departing again. The FAA put delays at an average of 47 minutes at that point. Individual flights could still be behind schedule after the hold ended.

Why flights stopped

The FAA attributed Tuesday’s disruption to the ground surveillance equipment. Monday’s pause at O’Hare involved staffing, and the November 13 nationwide ground stop involved a telecommunications problem. The public accounts of Tuesday’s radar issue provide no evidence of a cyberattack or AI involvement.
