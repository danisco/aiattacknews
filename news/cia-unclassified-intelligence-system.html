Unclassified CIA intelligence system breached, AP reports

The New York Times reported that the breach also affected the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency. Sources familiar with the intrusion suspected use of the AI tool ARTEX.

AI Attack News · Published November 24, 2026 · Updated November 25, 2026

A cyberattack reached an unclassified system the CIA uses to analyze information from public sources, the Associated Press reported on November 24. A person familiar with the incident told AP that the issue had been resolved and that no classified information was involved.

What the system handles

Analysts use open-source intelligence to make sense of material such as commercial satellite imagery, internet records and public reporting. The specific information exposed in this breach has not been publicly identified.

The Office of the Director of National Intelligence told Reuters its critical capabilities and classified systems continued to operate. Who carried out the attack, how they gained access and whether they used AI remain unknown.

November 24 evening update · What supports the AI claim?

Nextgov/FCW reported that ARTEX, an AI security tool, was suspected in the intelligence breach. Its report did not identify the source for that assessment or give technical evidence tying the tool to the affected system. Neither agency has publicly confirmed its use.

Two people familiar with a separate intrusion at the Defense Department’s Cyber Defense Command told the outlet that ARTEX was used there. One described the impact as limited. Whether the two intrusions were connected remains unknown.

November 25 update · A third intelligence agency

Two people familiar with the breach told The New York Times it also affected the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency, which analyzes imagery and geographic information. Some of the newspaper’s sources believed the intruders used ARTEX. The agencies have yet to release technical findings that establish how the attackers got in.
