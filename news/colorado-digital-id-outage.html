Colorado digital ID app stays offline as state investigates unauthorized access

Colorado estimates that data belonging to 50,000 to 150,000 people may be affected. Holiday travelers are being asked to bring a physical ID.

AI Attack News · Published November 25, 2026

Colorado is asking residents who rely on the myColorado app for identification to carry a physical license or state ID during Thanksgiving travel. State officials shut down the app after discovering on November 18 that someone had gained unauthorized access to platform data. In its November 24 update, the state gave no date for restoring service.

Whose information was affected?

The app has about 2.1 million users. Colorado estimates that the incident may have affected information belonging to 50,000 to 150,000 people. Investigators are still determining which records the person accessed. The state says it will contact affected individuals directly.

Colorado officials said they had seen no indication of a wider attack. The state’s account identifies no evidence of AI involvement.

How to reach state services

Residents can renew a license or vehicle registration through Colorado’s DMV portal. The state’s outage page also links to benefits, parks and immunization services that residents can reach outside the app.
