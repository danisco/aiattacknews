Colorado says myColorado breach involved documents from about 100,000 users

Files accessed included copies of passports, vehicle registrations and insurance cards. State officials said payment information was outside the breach.

AI Attack News · Published November 25, 2026

November 25 update · Which documents were accessed?

Colorado’s Office of Information Technology said the breach involved files belonging to roughly 100,000 myColorado users, KOAA reported Wednesday. Most of the files came from myDocuments, the part of the state app where residents can store their own records. Among the documents accessed were PDFs and photos of passports, vehicle registrations and insurance cards.

State officials said payment information was not involved. Investigators are matching the files to the people who uploaded them so the state can tell each affected user which documents were accessed. The state expects to start sending notices on November 27.

Why the app is offline

Colorado is asking residents who rely on the myColorado app for identification to carry a physical license or state ID during Thanksgiving travel. State officials shut down the app after discovering on November 18 that someone had gained unauthorized access to platform data. In its November 24 update, the state gave no date for restoring service.

The app has about 2.1 million users. Before Wednesday’s update, Colorado had estimated that the incident could have affected 50,000 to 150,000 people.

Colorado officials said they had seen no indication of a wider attack. The state’s account identifies no evidence of AI involvement.

How to reach state services

Residents can renew a license or vehicle registration through Colorado’s DMV portal. The state’s outage page also links to benefits, parks and immunization services that residents can reach outside the app.
