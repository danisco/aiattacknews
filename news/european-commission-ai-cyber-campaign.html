EU Academy shuts public access after theft of learners’ names and email addresses

The European Commission linked the leak to its earlier cyber campaign. It posted a notice for learners and is still investigating how many people were affected.

AI Attack News · Published November 23, 2026

November 23 update

People who use EU Academy for training lost public access to the platform after attackers took personal data, including names and email addresses. Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier said the agency warned users through the website. It had not sent individual emails by the time of Monday’s briefing.

Regnier linked the leak to the wider attack on Commission systems. Investigators had confirmed AI use in the campaign. They have yet to say who took the Academy data or what role AI played in that theft.

What the Commission disclosed on November 19

The Commission first detected the campaign on October 22 and temporarily took several applications offline. By November 19, most had returned to service.

Spokesperson Thomas Regnier said investigators confirmed the incident on October 26. The Commission contacted CERT-EU and the European Data Protection Supervisor. At the briefing, it acknowledged that a publicly available AI tool was involved.

November 23 update · Who investigators suspect

Euronews reported that two sources familiar with the investigation said a China-backed group was suspected. The outlet cited the attackers’ methods and reported use of an AI security tool called ARTEX. Those clues do not identify who operated the tool or establish government sponsorship. At Monday’s briefing, the Commission declined to assign responsibility while its investigation continues.

What investigators left open

Officials declined to identify the attacker or estimate the full scale. The public briefing establishes AI use but does not identify the tool or establish how much work it carried out independently.
