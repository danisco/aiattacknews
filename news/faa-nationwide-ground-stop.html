FAA ground stop halted U.S. departures. Here’s what caused it.

Thousands of travelers lost flights after a telecommunications failure. The FAA says the evidence points to a router problem and gives no indication of a cyberattack.

AI Attack News · Published November 23, 2026

November 13 · Departures stop

Around 3:30 p.m. Eastern, instability in the air traffic network prompted the FAA to stop departures nationwide. Aircraft already in the air could still land. The agency says about 3,500 did so safely. Traffic resumed at 8:15 p.m., with delays and cancellations continuing into Saturday.

November 14 · Airlines work through the backlog

The Transportation Department reported more than 8,000 flight operations after the stop lifted that night. On Saturday evening, American Airlines was still warning customers of delays and cancellations.

November 15 · The FAA explains the cause

The FAA traced the disruption to routine router replacement work and instability in legacy equipment. It counted roughly 12,462 cancellations on Friday across operators, while cautioning that some had other causes. Its public account identifies no evidence of malicious activity or AI involvement.
