FinCEN finds no evidence of breach in two financial reporting databases

An intruder reached some FinCEN systems. The bureau says investigators have found no evidence of access to its Bank Secrecy Act or beneficial ownership databases.

AI Attack News · Published November 24, 2026 · Updated November 25, 2026

November 25 update · Which records were at risk?

A FinCEN official told Reuters on Wednesday that investigators had found no evidence of a breach of either the Bank Secrecy Act database or the beneficial ownership database. The first holds reports that banks and other institutions file about financial activity. The second holds information on people who own or control certain companies.

Treasury is working with the FBI, the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency and outside forensic examiners to determine what the intruder reached. The investigation is ongoing.

The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, the Treasury bureau that collects reports on suspicious financial activity, said someone gained unauthorized access to some of its systems. It took systems offline as a precaution, according to an agency alert reported by The Verge on November 24.

What banks can still do

The bureau said online reporting remained available, although some services could be delayed. Financial institutions could still use its hotline to get urgent information to law enforcement. FinCEN has not identified the intruder or said which records were accessed. There is no public evidence of AI involvement.

Which deadlines changed?

In a separate November 24 order, FinCEN extended a deadline for financial institutions that used its Secure Data Transfer Mode as of November 18. They now have until December 30 to submit Bank Secrecy Act reports originally due from November 18 through December 29. They must still monitor for suspicious transactions. The order cites a filing disruption and does not specify what caused it.

The order also gives reporting companies required to file beneficial ownership reports and individuals required to update a FinCEN identifier until December 30 for submissions originally due in that same period.
