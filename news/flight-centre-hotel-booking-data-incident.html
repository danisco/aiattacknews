Flight Centre CEO estimates hotel booking breach exposed 500,000 names

Graham Turner said most files held only a name. Some passport numbers were taken, and existing hotel bookings remain valid.

AI Attack News · Published November 26, 2026 · Updated 8:58 p.m. Eastern

November 26 update · How many names were exposed?

Flight Centre chief executive Graham Turner estimated that the breach at an overseas accommodation supplier exposed about half a million names, according to The Courier-Mail. Most files contained only a customer’s name, he said. Turner did not give a count of individual travelers affected.

In a separate interview with ABC News Breakfast, Turner said some passport numbers were taken. He did not say how many. Flight Centre’s earlier stock exchange filing had described passport details as potentially exposed.

Flight Centre Travel Group is investigating the breach at a third-party hotel booking provider. The Australian group said in a November 26 stock exchange filing that customers can travel on their existing accommodation bookings. Flight bookings and corporate travel bookings are unaffected.

Which bookings are involved?

The system serves Flight Centre, Travel Associates, Cruiseabout and some independent agents. The company’s November 26 filing did not give a count of extracted records.

The company said most records it reviewed contained names, sometimes with basic travel information. A small fraction may have included limited passport details, birth dates, email addresses or phone numbers. Some records contained health or mobility information. Flight Centre said it had found no evidence of access to payment card or bank account details.

What travelers should watch for

Flight Centre said it would contact affected customers and advised travelers to watch for unexpected messages about their bookings. It warned against opening links or attachments or sharing passwords or payment details in response.

The group is working with the vendor and incident response firm Mandiant. It said credentials had been changed and monitoring increased. Flight Centre has yet to identify who was responsible or cite evidence of AI involvement.
