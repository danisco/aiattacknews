AI involvement suspected

GreyNoise finds data from 24 Check Point servers in campaign affecting at least 22 organizations

Researchers observed people directing the intrusions and suspect AI helped automate some tasks. They could not identify the operator or its motive.

AI Attack News · Published November 24, 2026

GreyNoise said it found stolen data and records of commands from 24 Check Point security management servers. These are systems organizations use to manage their network defenses. The researchers traced the activity to at least 22 organizations, including ones in government, financial services, energy and transportation.

Why researchers suspect AI use

In its November 23 report, GreyNoise described recurring automated tasks along with actions it could see a person directing. The researchers said the timing and behavior led them to suspect the operator used AI to help carry out the campaign. They did not identify a particular model or establish which decisions it made.

Where the evidence ends

GreyNoise calls the campaign GT13. It confirmed activity from October 17 through November 2, when its visibility ended. The company said it was working with government partners and response teams to notify victims. It did not attribute the campaign to a country or identify a motive.
