Peninsula Energy shares suspended after Wyoming uranium shutdown

Peninsula expects to update investors before Australian trading opens November 30. Its Wyoming plant and wells still need safety checks before production can resume.

AI Attack News · Published November 25, 2026 · Updated November 26, 2026

November 26 update · When shareholders can expect more information

The Australian Securities Exchange suspended trading in Peninsula Energy shares on November 25 at the company’s request. The suspension followed a two-day trading halt after a cybersecurity incident shut down its Lance uranium operation in Wyoming.

Peninsula said it expected to tell investors before the market opens Monday, November 30, when production could resume and whether the shutdown would affect its production forecast. The company has yet to give a date for restarting the plant or its wells.

Peninsula Energy shut down its Lance uranium operation in Wyoming after its U.S. subsidiary, Strata Energy, discovered a cybersecurity incident on November 20. The problem reached the systems that control the processing plant and wells, Peninsula told the Australian Securities Exchange in a November 25 filing.

What has to happen before the restart?

Strata isolated the affected systems. Its operations staff and outside specialists are checking that the controls can run the plant and wells safely. Peninsula said it would give a further update on timing in the coming days. It was too early to say whether the shutdown would change its 2027 production forecast.

The company said payroll, finance and other administrative systems in the United States and Australia were unaffected. It notified U.S. authorities and is investigating whether any data was affected. The filing identifies neither an attacker nor any evidence that AI was used.
