Microsoft sets one-hour recovery goal after outage left customers without email

Internal memos reviewed by Reuters describe a “Code Red” effort to shorten future outages. Microsoft traced the November 12–13 disruption to a Windows defect.

AI Attack News · Published November 24, 2026

Microsoft wants its engineers to restore service worldwide within an hour of a future major failure, according to internal memos Reuters reported on November 24. During the November outage, investigators struggled to piece together diagnostic information scattered across different systems. The company plans to use AI tools to help engineers read those signals more quickly.

What stopped working

For nearly 24 hours, people who relied on Exchange Online had trouble opening mailboxes or getting messages delivered. Features in Teams and Copilot that depended on Exchange also failed for some users. Reuters reported that government offices in Australia, Japan and the United States were affected, trading firms faced delays and airlines lost access to some communications.

Why recovery took so long

Microsoft’s incident review traces the outage to a Windows software defect introduced in July on servers that direct traffic to Exchange. Under heavy load those servers began failing. Engineers had to identify the defect, restart servers to install a fix and restart them again to activate it. Microsoft recorded customer impact from 16:20 UTC on November 12 to 16:10 UTC on November 13.

Microsoft ruled out both a cyberattack and AI agents as the cause. The one-hour target described in the memos applies to future incidents. The “Code Red” work began after service had recovered.
