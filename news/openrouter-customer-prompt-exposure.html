OpenRouter removes three AI providers after investigation into customer prompts

MiniMax says its monitoring software opened links from some requests marked for zero data retention. Phala separately identified 152,092 requests at risk on one DeepSeek route.

AI Attack News · Published November 23, 2026

OpenRouter, a service that sends customers’ AI requests to model providers, said on November 22 that it removed three providers after researchers found evidence that some services mishandled prompt data. The company said the investigation found no unauthorized access to OpenRouter itself.

November 24 update · Why links in MiniMax requests were opened

MiniMax said on Tuesday that its own security software logged web addresses from some requests forwarded by OpenRouter and other providers, including requests marked for zero data retention. The software later visited some of those addresses. That explains why a link placed in a prompt could register a visit after the model had finished responding.

MiniMax said its monitoring logs did not keep entire conversations. In the requests it examined, it found no evidence that outsiders received prompt text or responses beyond the reported link activity. The company said it disabled the checks on the affected business route and deleted the logged URLs. Its findings concern MiniMax traffic. Phala and Together AI reported separate incidents involving outside access.

November 24 update · Which Phala requests were exposed?

In its final report on November 23, Phala narrowed the exposure to requests that OpenRouter sent to Phala for the DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 model between October 21 at 02:25 UTC and November 1 at 02:03 UTC. If a request you sent on that route contained a password, token or API key, Phala advises replacing that credential.

Phala counted 152,092 requests that reached the affected server during that window. A researcher traced the misuse of decoy credentials back to October requests, but the investigation could not determine how many of the requests the intruder actually read. Phala said removing the replacement route as a precaution caused no outage for OpenRouter users.

How customer messages got out at Together AI

Together AI, one of the providers that disclosed an incident, said its own coding agent accidentally published a credential. Before the company could secure it, an outsider used it to download stored samples of requests customers had sent to AI models in June and July. Together AI said it was still checking whether those messages contained sensitive personal information.

The outsider tested credentials found in the samples. Five were decoys planted by researchers at Dragos. In the broader investigation, Dragos also saw one attacker use an AI tool to check a few keys already in hand. The evidence does not establish that AI was used to steal Together AI’s prompts.

What changed for customers

OpenRouter removed 125 endpoints from the list available to customers who require providers to discard their data. Some removals were precautionary. OpenRouter did not name the three providers it dropped or say how many customers had data taken.
