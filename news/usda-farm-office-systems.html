Some USDA farm office systems return after cybersecurity shutdown

County offices resumed some loan and conservation work. USDA has yet to say whether the disruption delayed payments to individual farmers.

AI Attack News · Published November 24, 2026

Some U.S. Department of Agriculture county offices could again handle loan and conservation work on Monday, after a cybersecurity incident took systems offline the previous week. DTN reported on November 24 that local offices had regained access to conservation software, while staff were told that other systems still needed to be restored.

What this means for farmers

The disruption affected software used for farm payments as well as loans. That left county employees unable to carry out much of their usual work with farmers. USDA has not said which individual payments or loans, if any, ran late. Nor has it given a date for full recovery.

What USDA has confirmed

Richard Fordyce, the undersecretary responsible for farm production and conservation, told farm broadcasters that USDA shut systems down in response to a cyber threat. He said no data was lost. The department has not publicly established whether anyone accessed or copied farmers’ records. No public evidence links the incident to AI.
