Wiz traces theft of private code to an AI-assisted cloud attack

Researchers linked activity at more than a dozen organizations to the campaign. At one, attackers stole private code and credentials and gained administrator access.

AI Attack News · Published November 24, 2026

Attackers stole private software repositories and credentials from an organization during a campaign that used ARTEX, an AI security tool, according to an investigation Wiz published on November 23. The attackers also gained administrator access to a cluster of computers that ran the organization’s applications.

What connects the attacks

Wiz calls the campaign ARTEX-Harvester. From late October to mid-November, its researchers saw recurring task numbers, software signatures and overlapping network activity across more than a dozen organizations. Wiz said its confidence in the connection varied from case to case.

That distinction matters for the people whose information might be at risk. Wiz documented stolen code and credentials at one organization. Its report did not name that organization, identify customers whose data was taken or establish that every organization it counted suffered a breach.

What investigators know about the operator

Wiz attributed the campaign to an operator using AI tools. It did not identify the person or group directing the activity. The report also does not establish how much of the confirmed theft the software carried out on its own.
